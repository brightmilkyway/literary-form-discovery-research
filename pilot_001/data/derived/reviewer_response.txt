ISSUE 1 — THE SENTENCE SPLITTER DID NOT REALLY SPLIT SENTENCES   [ACCEPTED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Raised: the unit printed as "sentence 534" in verbatim_echo_test.txt contained
three separate speeches, so the whole sentence-scale analysis is suspect.

Confirmed.  Diagnosis:

  nltk.sent_tokenize(body)            764 units, 152 of them containing an
                                      internal line break (UNDER-split)
  cause                               the text writes '.”' (period + closing
                                      CURLY quote) before a blank line, and
                                      punkt does not treat '.”' as a sentence
                                      end, so consecutive speeches were merged

First fix attempt — normalise curly quotes to straight — was WRONG and made
things worse in the other direction.  Measured on probe sentences:

  “Is it snowing again, Mr Conroy?” asked Lily.
      curly    -> 1 part  (correct)
      straight -> 2 parts (over-split: the dialogue tag cut off its speech)

Second fix, adopted in ``code/sentences.py``: split on blank lines first (the
text's own paragraph structure), then punkt inside each paragraph on the
ORIGINAL curly-quoted text.

  split_sentences(body)               1023 units, 0 under-split, 0 over-split
  offset round-trip failures          0
  units ending in an abbreviation     0
  units starting with a lowercase dialogue tag   0  (was 50 under the
                                                  straight-quote attempt)

Spot-check (``spotcheck_sentences.py``, full text in
``data/derived/sentence_spotcheck.txt``): a random sample of 30 units, the full
context around the echo sentences, and every flagged pattern are printed for
inspection.  The remaining flags are benign: 67 units end with a colon because
the paragraph break falls before the quoted speech they introduce; 23 units are
genuine short exclamations ("Julia!", "“West Briton!”").

Consequences for the results — everything at sentence scale was re-run:

  * the echo sentences are now [728] and [1018], not [534] and [759];
  * the echo result is STRONGER, not weaker:
        MiniLM cos 0.726 (was 0.581), mutual rank 1/1 of 1023
        bge    cos 0.757,               mutual rank 1/1 of 1023
        TF-IDF cos 0.510,               mutual rank 1/1 of 1023
        control: 200 random distant pairs, MiniLM max 0.437, bge max 0.720
        -> the echo exceeds the MAXIMUM of the random control in both models
  * TextTiling orthogonality at sentence scale: percentiles 41-53%
    (was 45-52% with the broken splitter) -> unchanged conclusion
  * sentence-scale NCD length confound: r = +0.661, 43.7% of variance
    (was +0.728 / 53.1%) -> the earlier claim that the confound is WORSE at
    sentence scale is withdrawn; it is comparable to the 150-word value
    (+0.689 / 47.5%)

ISSUE 2a — NCD ASSUMES C(xy) = C(yx)   [ACCEPTED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
``ncd.py`` computes C(xy) once and mirrors it.  Compressors do not satisfy
C(xy) = C(yx), so the symmetry was an assumption.

Measured on group150 (n=78), both directions computed independently:

  r(forward, reverse)                      +0.951
  |NCD(xy) - NCD(yx)|                      mean 0.0076, max 0.0349
  pairs changed by more than 0.01          890 of 3003  (29.6%)
  top-10 closest pairs, overlap            6/10

So the order matters for the pairs one would actually report.  The matrix
remains symmetric by construction for comparability, but this is now documented
as an assumption, and the reverse order is reported alongside.

ISSUE 2b — "NCD IS A FORMULAIC-TEMPLATE DETECTOR" IS OVERCLAIMED   [ACCEPTED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The claim rested on top pairs that share a sentence frame, but sentence-scale
NCD is strongly length-confounded.  Two checks:

  (i)  the 50 closest raw-NCD pairs have a mean length difference of 1.4 words,
       against 11.1 words for all non-adjacent pairs -> the closest pairs are
       strongly length-matched;
  (ii) the equal-length test the report itself proposed: within bins of
       sentences with the SAME word count (>= 25 sentences per bin, 17 bins),
       compare frame-sharing pairs against non-frame pairs.

       result: frame-sharing pairs are more similar in only 9 of 17 bins, and
       the differences are tiny (|delta| < 0.03 in most bins).

  => Once length is held constant, sharing a dialogue-tag frame does NOT
     predict NCD similarity.  The "formulaic-template detector" claim is
     WITHDRAWN.  What survives is weaker and is stated as such: at sentence
     scale NCD's closest pairs are near-repetitions, but those pairs are also
     length-matched, and the two effects are confounded in the present data.

  The verbatim lyric repetition ("For they are jolly gay fellows," x3, d=0.062
  against 0.26 for the runner-up) is a genuine exception: NCD is not blind to
  content, it is dominated by length.

ISSUE 3 — THE 0.83 "FORM BASELINE" IS A PLACEHOLDER ARTEFACT   [ACCEPTED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Replacing every content word with the same token "thing" manufactures a large
number of identical tokens across all passages, so the observed rise in cosine
measures the shared placeholder, not shared form.  The "form baseline = 0.83"
interpretation is RETRACTED.

Corrected design — delete, do not replace:

  form-only    = the passage with every content word DELETED
  content-only = the passage with every function word DELETED

No artificial token is introduced.  Applied to 120 random distant pairs:

  mean cos_orig 0.475 (sd 0.100)
  mean cos_form 0.712 (sd 0.074)
  mean cos_content 0.413 (sd 0.106)

  r(cos_orig, cos_form)    = +0.180
  r(cos_orig, cos_content) = +0.782

  => Form similarity is HIGH but nearly constant: it does not discriminate
     between pairs, and it is best read as the "both passages are English
     prose" prior rather than a stylistic match.
  => The variation in the original cosine tracks CONTENT, not form
     (r = +0.78 vs +0.18).

This also reverses the direction of the withdrawn interpretation.  The pilot
had claimed the encoder largely reads form; under the corrected design it reads
content, while NCD is the method that is dominated by length.  That contrast
is cleaner and is what the revised report states.

ISSUE 4 — THE MOTIF-SET TEST DOES NOT SHOW SNOW IS SPECIAL   [ACCEPTED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The shape-matched null gives ~100th percentile to weather AND to music,
religion, death and food/drink, so it cannot support a claim about snow in
particular.  Confirmed on the corrected sentences (1023 units):

  shape-matched null percentiles, band >= 5 units
     weather     100.0 /  98.2 / 100.0   (MiniLM / bge / TF-IDF)
     music       100.0 / 100.0 / 100.0
     religion    100.0 /  98.2 / 100.0
     death       100.0 / 100.0 / 100.0
     food_drink   99.8 /  38.6 / 100.0

The set-level test is therefore reported as what it is: evidence that these
lexical sets are organised at sentence scale, NOT evidence that the weather
material is more organised than any other motif.  The snow-specific evidence in
this pilot is the verbatim echo (mutual rank 1/1 in both encoders), and nothing
else.
