================================================================================
ROUND 4 SUMMARY — controlled experiments on the two surviving claims
================================================================================

Two claims survived the v3 adjudication:
  (A) the "song" cluster is the only cross-model, cross-method finding;
  (B) the final snow passage is the most peripheral unit in both encoders.

This round asked, for each, WHAT the finding actually rests on, using
controlled substitution (ablate a word class / transplant it) with matched
random-ablation controls.  Both claims survived, but in a REVISED form, and one
control failed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1 — WHERE DOES THE FINAL SNOW PASSAGE'S PERIPHERALITY COME FROM?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: data/derived/snow_source_experiment.txt

Three hypotheses: H1 lexical field (snow/west/death), H2 form/register,
H3 length.  Method: modify unit 77 only, re-encode, measure its mean cosine to
the fixed embeddings of the other 77 units; compare against control units
(other pure-narration passages) and matched random ablations.

  H1 REJECTED.  Ablating the winter/west/death lexicon (37 word types) moves
     centrality to the 62nd percentile of a matched random ablation of 37
     content-word types — in BOTH models.  It is indistinguishable from
     ablating arbitrary content.
     Direction test: transplanting that lexicon into three more central
     passages (units 63, 57, 59) does not reliably make them peripheral
     (one small drop of three in each model, inconsistent).
     => The finale is NOT peripheral because of its famous imagery.

  H2 WEAKLY SUPPORTED.  Destroying within-sentence word order (bag-of-words)
     raises centrality in both models (MiniLM 0.383 -> 0.397; bge 0.631 ->
     0.683, rank 1 -> 11).  But the effect is not unique to unit 77, so it
     indicates that *ordered form* carries part of the signal, not that unit
     77 is special in its syntax.

  H3 REJECTED.  Unit 77 is 160 words, mid-range; the control units span
     152-217 words with no relation to peripherality (SBERT x length r = 0.027).

  NET: the peripherality is a distributed property of the passage's overall
  profile.  No single removable component explains it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2 — THE 'SONG' CLUSTER: LITERAL REPETITION, OR A DISCURSIVE THREAD?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: data/derived/song_cluster_analysis.txt

  (a) The cluster is NOT "the music units".  Music-word density rank:
      unit 54 = 3rd highest density (5.60/100w) but NOT in the cluster;
      unit 56 = 0.00 density (rank 44/78) but IS in the cluster.
      => the cluster is not vocabulary-driven.

  (b) LITERAL REPETITION REJECTED.  Removing the sung lyric from unit 55
      changes cos(55,58) by 0.000 — it sits at the 100th percentile of a
      matched random-deletion control (mean 0.616, range [0.599, 0.658]).
      Removing the song title changes it by +0.010 (slightly UP).
      Removing music vocabulary: +0.001.
      => neither the lyric nor the title holds the pair together.

  (c) The cluster is really TWO SUB-CLUSTERS, bridged by one unit.
      Mutual-rank table (correction-free, MiniLM; "worse of the two ranks",
      lower = stronger):
        (72,73) 2 | (55,58) 2 | (55,56) 3 | (56,58) 6 | (69,72) 9
        (49,72) 14 | (49,73) 17 | (55,72) 18 | (37,55) 23 | all others 40+
      Sub-cluster A "the moment of HEARING"  = {54, 55, 56, 58}
        (paras 316-351: Gabriel watches Gretta on the stairs listening to the
         distant voice; the lyric is heard; the singing stops; the song is
         named; the good-nights are said)
      Sub-cluster B "the moment of TELLING"  = {69, 72, 73}
        (paras 408-447: Michael Furey, Galway, the wet window, the rain,
         the burial in Oughterard)
      The BRIDGE is unit 69, the only unit that names both the song and the
      person: "I am thinking about a person long ago who used to sing that
      song ... named Michael Furey. He used to sing that song, The Lass of
      Aughrim."
      => What is robust computationally is not "the song" as a textual object
         but the pairing of two scenes and the single sentence that joins them.

  (d) FAILED CONTROL, reported as such.  Character-name ablation lowers
      cos(55,58) by 0.076, below the matched control range — but a null sample
      of 40 random distant pairs shows the same effect in 82% of cases
      (mean relative drop 2.6%).  So "the same cast" cannot be shown to be
      specific to this pair.  => data/derived/name_ablation_control.txt

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3 — THE WEATHER/SNOW MATERIAL AS A SET: INVISIBLE TO ALL SIX METHODS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: data/derived/weather_set_test.txt

Define W by content only (>=2 hits of snow/rain/wet/window/goloshes/
shivering): 13 units.  Core set W_core (snow>=3 or goloshes>=5 or
rain+wet+window+shivering>=4): 6 units {2, 8, 41, 56, 73, 77}.

Permutation test (random sets of the same size, 10,000 / 5,000 draws):

  method        within-W  W-to-rest   percentile | within-core  percentile
  TF-IDF          0.128     0.119       80.0%    |   0.120       44.4%
  Jaccard         0.143     0.137       71.5%    |   0.130       24.6%
  MiniLM          0.513     0.492       67.8%    |   0.504       53.3%
  bge             0.700     0.699       24.9%    |   0.680       12.3%
  NCD(1-d)        0.126     0.124       59.2%    |   0.133       72.5%
  NCD-resid      -0.002    -0.002       41.1%    |  -0.007       24.1%

  => No method finds the weather set more cohesive than a random set of the
     same size.  Percentiles straddle 50% (12-80%); bge is BELOW chance.

Supporting facts:
  * The one VERBATIM echo in the text — "the snow is general all over Ireland"
    (unit 56, Mary Jane quoting the newspaper) vs "snow was general all over
    Ireland" (unit 77, Gabriel) — is recovered by NO method.  Mutual rank of
    (56,77): MiniLM 17, bge 20, TF-IDF 35, Jaccard 53, NCD 42, NCD-resid 58.
  * But from unit 77's own side, TF-IDF's top partners are exactly
    (2, 0.132), (73, 0.128), (56, 0.128) — the snow/rain passages.  The link
    is real but one-directional and weak.
  * The (56,77) pair does appear at rank 3 in bge's hub-corrected top-20 —
    an artifact of unit 77 being the most peripheral unit (periphery
    inflation, see the v3 hub-correction flaw).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS MEANS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The pilot's one robust positive finding is NOT "the snow motif" and NOT
   "the song as text".  It is the pairing of the hearing scene and the
   telling scene, joined by Gretta's single sentence.  That is a *scene*
   structure, and it is the only thing that survives every control.
2. The material that literary criticism has always treated as the story's
   structural centre — the snow — is the material that all six computational
   representations are weakest on: it forms no detectable set, its one
   verbatim echo is recovered by nobody, and the finale's distinctiveness
   does not come from its lexicon.
3. That asymmetry is the most interesting result of the pilot, and it is
   falsifiable: if the snow structure were real at this scale, at least one
   representation should have shown a set-level effect.  None did.
