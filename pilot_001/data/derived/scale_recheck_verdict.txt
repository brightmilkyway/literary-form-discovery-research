RESULT 1 — TEXTTILING'S ORTHOGONALITY IS SCALE-ROBUST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same test as at 150 words: are the adjacent-sentence similarities at
TextTiling's boundaries lower than at non-boundaries?  (50% = no relation.)

  variant (a) word-based TextTiling (w=20, k=6), 130 boundaries
      MiniLM 52%   bge 48%   TF-IDF 52%   Jaccard 50%
  variant (b) sentence-based TextTiling (token sequences = sentences)
      k=4,  137 boundaries : 51% / 50% / 45% / 46%
      k=6,  123 boundaries : 47% / 50% / 46% / 48%
      k=10, 126 boundaries : 48% / 49% / 48% / 47%

All percentiles lie in 45-52%.  The 150-word result was 47-56%.

  => VERDICT: orthogonality is NOT a scale artifact.  TextTiling's lexical
     cohesion signal really is unrelated to what SBERT / TF-IDF / Jaccard
     measure, at both scales and under both adaptations of the algorithm.
     Candidate 4 stands unchanged.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 2 — NCD'S LENGTH CONFOUND IS WORSE AT SENTENCE SCALE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  r(NCD distance, compressed-size difference)
      150-word units : +0.689      (size alone explains 47.5% of variance)
      sentences      : +0.728      (size alone explains 53.1% of variance)
  r(NCD distance, index distance)
      150-word units : +0.155
      sentences      : -0.012
  residual fix (regress out the size term):
      sentences      : D = 0.7078 + 0.001064 * |size_i - size_j|, r -> 0.000

  => VERDICT: the confound is not a 150-word artifact; it is worse at finer
     scale, as expected for short strings.  Candidate 1 stands and is
     strengthened: at sentence scale NCD is unusable without length
     correction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 3 (NEW) — AT SENTENCE SCALE, NCD DETECTS SYNTACTIC TEMPLATES, NOT CONTENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The verbatim snow echo (sentences 534 and 759, 225 sentences apart):

  method      similarity   mutual rank from sentence 534 (of 764)
  MiniLM        0.581      1        <- the echo is the top match
  bge           0.761      1
  TF-IDF        0.447      1
  Jaccard       0.186      4
  NCD (raw)     0.759 dist 123,543 / 263,175 pairs   <- bottom half
  NCD (residual)-0.0918     10,866 / 263,175 pairs   <- top 4% after the fix

  NCD does NOT find it, even after the length fix.

Why not: NCD's top-10 closest non-adjacent sentence pairs are near-formulaic
repetitions of the SAME SENTENCE TEMPLATE:

  d=0.389  0 & 15    "Lily, the caretaker's daughter, was literally run off her
                      feet."  /  "Lily, the caretaker's daughter, did
                      housemaid's work for them."
  d=0.405  257 & 332  "'O, do go, Gabriel,' she cried." /
                      "'Where is Gabriel?' she cried."
  d=0.426  637 & 651  "If she would only turn to him or come to him of her own
                      accord!" / "Just when he was wishing for it she had come
                      to him of her own accord."
  d=0.441  34 & 529   "She had preceded him into the pantry to help him off
                      with his overcoat." / "He went into the pantry hastily
                      and put on his overcoat."
  d=0.453  140 & 523  "'Three ladies, Mary Jane,' said Aunt Kate." /
                      "'O do, Mary Jane,' said Aunt Kate."
  d=0.460  502 & 601  "He was in a dark part of the hall gazing up the
                      staircase." / "An old man was dozing in a great hooded
                      chair in the hall."

  => VERDICT (new candidate, methodological): at sentence scale NCD behaves
     as a *formulaic-template detector*.  It finds sentences built on the same
     frame ("X, she cried"; "said Aunt Kate"; "in the hall") and misses a
     verbatim content repetition whose two members have different frames.
     This is consistent with the "form baseline" result: compression sees
     form.  It also explains why NCD and the embeddings agree so little
     (r = 0.317 at 150 words).
