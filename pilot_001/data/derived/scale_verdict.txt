WHAT WAS CLAIMED IN v4
----------------------
"Snow/weather material does not form a set under any of six representations
(permutation percentiles 12-80%); the text's one verbatim echo is recovered by
no method."  -> concluded: the structure criticism cares about most is the one
computation cannot see.

WHAT THE SCALE TEST FOUND
-------------------------
Three test designs were tried at sentence scale (764 sentences) and paragraph
scale (455).  The first two failed for instructive reasons; the third is sound.

  ATTEMPT 1 — permutation null (random sets of the same size).
    Result: weather 99.9 / 96.9 / 100.0 % (MiniLM / bge / TF-IDF) ...
    ... but so was EVERY set: sets defined by a single random content word
    scored at the median 99.2 / 95.4 / 100.0, and the contiguous-block
    sensitivity check scored LOWER (87.8 / 2.0 / 64.0).
    => the null is saturated; the test cannot discriminate.  DISCARDED.

  ATTEMPT 2 — matched word-set null (300 single-word sets, size-matched).
    Result: weather at the 0.0th percentile, z = -4.3 / -4.3 / -5.3
    => looks like "weather is LESS cohesive than a typical word set".
    BUT: a single word's occurrences are concentrated in one scene, while the
    weather lexicon is spread over the whole text; a spread-out set has all
    its pairs far apart.  The null is biased against diffuse motifs, and
    "music" scored even lower (z = -10.8 / -32.1) purely for being diffuse.
    DISCARDED.

  ATTEMPT 3 — shape-matched null (SOUND).
    Keep the same number of runs and the same run lengths, place them at
    random non-overlapping positions; 500 draws.  This controls for size, run
    count, run length and dispersion, leaving only *which* units are picked.
    Score = mean over within-set pairs of (similarity - mean at that distance)
            / sd at that distance   (removes the adjacency effect).

    SENTENCE scale, band >= 5 units apart:
      set            n  runs | MiniLM  obs/null/pct | bge            | TF-IDF
      weather       51   40  | 0.49  0.02  100.0%  | 0.35  0.00  99.0% | 0.70 0.00 100.0%
      music         85   58  | 0.77  0.00  100.0%  | 0.34  0.00  99.8% | 0.58 -0.00 100.0%

CORRECTED CONCLUSION
--------------------
1. RETRACTED: "the snow material is invisible to all six representations".
   That was an artifact of the 150-word analysis unit.
2. The verbatim echo is not merely recoverable - it is the strongest
   sentence-level match in the text, in both models.
3. The weather lexicon is a normal, functional motif at sentence scale; it
   coheres significantly against a shape-matched null.
4. What is true at 150 words is DILUTION: a 150-word unit containing this
   sentence also contains ~140 other words, and the signal disappears.
   The pilot's original 150-word permutation test (percentiles 12-80%) was
   therefore measuring dilution, not the absence of structure.

METHODOLOGICAL CONSEQUENCE FOR THE WHOLE PILOT
----------------------------------------------
The 150-word "group150" unit, chosen in the main run for cross-method
comparability, has a systematic blind spot: it destroys signals carried by a
single sentence.  Every negative result obtained at that scale must be
re-examined at sentence scale before it is reported as a fact about the text.
This applies to:
  * the "snow does not form a set" result      -> RETRACTED here
  * the "no method recovers the verbatim echo" -> RETRACTED here
  * the (2,73) verdict                          -> UNAFFECTED (it failed a
    cross-model test at its own scale, and the real snow link turns out to be
    the verbatim phrase, not the threshold/weather schema)
  * the TextTiling-vs-similarity orthogonality  -> UNAFFECTED (measured at
    150 words for both sides; but worth re-running at sentence scale)
